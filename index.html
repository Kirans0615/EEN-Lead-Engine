The Luxury Wholesale OS

A system for finding $700K–$2M+ sellers in Maryland before they ever talk to an agent — built on public records nobody else mines, life-event signals nobody else stacks, and a student taskforce nobody else has. Anything under the luxury floor routes to EEN's standard operation; this system hunts only Private Client product.

How this system works — and why luxury is a different sport

Entry-level wholesaling is a volume game: mass lists, postcards, thin fees, deep buyer pools. Luxury wholesaling inverts every one of those. The seller demands discretion and competence signals, not urgency. The buyer pool is ten builders deep, not a thousand investors deep — so dispo is built before lead gen, never after. And the fee is large enough to be visible, so exit structure (double close / novation) is chosen per deal, not defaulted. Every module below is tuned to those three realities.

The workflow runs in five stages: a lead is sourced by one of the eight engines, stacked against other motivation signals, scored with luxury-weighted signals, put into a white-glove sequence matched to its situation, and priced against a named buyer in the Buyer Network — with Maryland § 10-715 compliance checked before any contract.

Engine 01

Probate Pipeline

The highest-converting luxury list in Maryland — estates in administration where the personal representative needs to liquidate a $700K+ property, often from out of state.

▸ The Plan — how it works

Why nobody else finds these: probate leads never hit the MLS or list-broker software. They only exist in Register of Wills filings that must be pulled and cross-referenced by hand — free to access, expensive in labor. EEN's intern taskforce makes the labor cost near zero.

  1. Weekly pull (intern): search new estate filings in Montgomery, Howard & Anne Arundel counties on the Register of Wills estate search.
  2. Cross-reference (intern): look up each decedent's name in SDAT Real Property to find owned property. Keep only parcels assessed $600K+.
  3. Qualify: flag estates where the personal representative's mailing address is out of county/state — distance = motivation to sell as-is.
  4. Skip trace the personal representative and enter the lead in the Pipeline Tracker with source "Probate."
  5. Sequence: send the handwritten-style condolence-appropriate letter (Marketing Sequences → Template A). Never cold-call a fresh estate.

Engine 02

Teardown Permit Radar

Mines Montgomery County demolition & new-construction permits to find two things at once: the builders who are your cash buyers, and the neighbors who are your next sellers.

▸ The Plan — how it works

The innovation is "permit adjacency": when a builder tears down one 1950s rambler on a street in Bethesda 20817, every similar dated house on that street just became a proven teardown lot with a proven buyer — before any list software knows it. You market to the neighbors of construction sites.

  1. Monthly pull (intern): export demolition + new single-family construction permits from dataMontgomery for zips 20814/15/16/17, 20854, 20895.
  2. Buyer side (founder): the permit applicant is an active cash builder — call them, get their buy-box, add to the dispo list. Ten builder relationships = guaranteed exits.
  3. Seller side (intern): pull every same-vintage house within 2 blocks of each permit from SDAT; filter to 20+ year ownership.
  4. Mail the adjacency letter: "A builder just paid $X for the lot at [street]. We can get you a comparable cash offer without listing." (Template B variant.)
  5. Track which streets produce responses; double down on active corridors.

Engine 03

Long-Tenure Equity Map

Owners who bought in Potomac or Chevy Chase 25–50 years ago, hold near-100% equity, and want out of a 4,000 sq ft house — without staging, showings, or strangers walking through.

▸ The Plan — how it works

Everyone filters for "high equity." Almost nobody stacks tenure + age signals + lot value, because it takes parcel-level research. A 1978 purchase date plus a homeowner's tax credit application is a downsizing conversation waiting to happen — and these owners answer professional letters, not postcards.

  1. Quarterly build (intern): in the target zips, list parcels with transfer dates before 2000 and assessed value $700K+ via SDAT.
  2. Refine: flag parcels where land value is a high share of total assessment (teardown economics) and where the dwelling is original/dated (street view check).
  3. Signal boost: cross-check the senior/homeowners' tax credit rolls — an application signals fixed income in an expensive house.
  4. Mail Template B (the discreet downsizer letter) on heavy stock, signed by a named co-founder, 5–7 touches over 6 months.
  5. Offer both paths: cash as-is now, or quiet off-market sale on their timeline. Certainty is the product.

Engine 04

Silent Distress Signals

Wealthy-zip distress that never looks like distress: property tax delinquency on $1M+ homes, lis pendens filings on jumbo mortgages, and HOA/mechanic's liens in land records.

▸ The Plan — how it works

Foreclosure lists are picked over at the entry level, but almost no wholesaler works the luxury end — the list is short, the addresses are intimidating, and the owners demand discretion. That's the moat. A Potomac owner two years behind on taxes will never call a "we buy houses" number, but they will take a confidential meeting.

  1. Annual + monthly (intern): pull the county tax sale list each spring; filter to assessed value $700K+. Monitor monthly for new lis pendens in Case Search.
  2. Land records (intern): search mdlandrec.net for HOA liens and mechanic's liens recorded against target-zip parcels.
  3. Stack: any parcel with 2+ distress signals goes straight to the Hot tier.
  4. Founder-only outreach: these owners get a personal, private letter and a direct phone line — never a mass-mail piece. Frame: "solve it quietly, on your terms, before deadlines force worse options."
  5. Move fast: tax sale and foreclosure timelines are fixed; speed-to-lead decides who wins these.

Engine 05

Expired Luxury Recon

Luxury listings sit 60+ days and expire at far higher rates than the rest of the market. Every expired or withdrawn $800K+ listing is a seller who already proved motivation — and just failed publicly.

▸ The Plan — how it works

Expired data lives in Bright MLS, which wholesalers can't access — so they skip it. EEN's move: partner with agents instead of competing. The agent keeps a payday, the seller gets a quiet exit, EEN gets deal flow nobody mails.

  1. Build the agent alliance (founder): identify 10–15 listing agents in the target zips carrying stale $800K+ inventory (public portals show days-on-market).
  2. The pitch: "When your listing expires or your seller is done with showings, bring it to us — we'll make a cash/as-is offer and you stay in the deal as the referring or dual-fee agent."
  3. Monitor (intern): track 90+ day listings weekly on public portals as the early-warning list.
  4. Direct mail: once a listing visibly disappears without a sale record, send Template C ("your listing expired — there's a private alternative").
  5. Long game: consider one co-founder getting licensed for direct MLS/expired access and legitimacy.

Engine 06

Absentee & Vacancy Sweep

Luxury homes whose owners are somewhere else: SDAT mailing address ≠ property address, USPS-flagged vacancies, code-enforcement complaints, and deferred maintenance spotted on drive-bys.

▸ The Plan — how it works

Driving for dollars is a $150K-neighborhood tactic that nobody applies to Potomac — interns with a route map and a phone camera change that. A neglected $1.2M colonial with an out-of-state tax bill is one of the strongest signals in existence, and it appears on no purchasable list with this freshness.

  1. List build (intern): filter SDAT for target-zip parcels where the owner's mailing address is out of state or a trust/LLC with a distant address.
  2. Vacancy check: cross-reference HUD's USPS vacancy dataset by census tract; flag matches.
  3. Drive routes (interns, weekly): assigned street routes through target neighborhoods logging deferred maintenance — tarped roofs, dead lawns, full mailboxes, unshoveled snow.
  4. Code complaints (intern): pull dataMontgomery code-enforcement cases in target zips — a violation on a luxury home means an overwhelmed or absent owner.
  5. Stack & mail: absentee + visual distress = Hot tier; absentee only = standard 6-touch sequence.

Engine 07

Divorce Docket Miner

Contested divorces in high-asset households almost always force a house sale — and both parties usually want it done fast, private, and without an open-house circus.

▸ The Plan — how it works

Divorce filings are public in Maryland Case Search, but matching a case to a $1M+ marital home takes name-by-name SDAT cross-referencing that list vendors don't do at the county level. Handled with total discretion, this is a consistently underworked source.

  1. Monthly pull (intern): new absolute-divorce filings in Montgomery & Howard County Circuit Courts via Case Search.
  2. Cross-reference: match party names against SDAT ownership in target zips; keep joint-owned parcels $700K+.
  3. Wait 60–90 days after filing before first contact — raw filings are too fresh; mid-process is when the sale decision lands.
  4. Tone-matched letter: neutral, addressed to each party separately: "If selling the home becomes part of your next chapter, we buy privately — no showings, no signs, flexible timing."
  5. Better path (founder): build referral relationships with 5–10 family-law attorneys in Bethesda/Rockville — one attorney can outproduce the whole list.

Live Intel Real data

These are not mockups. The three tools below query Maryland's live government data systems directly from this page — the same records the engines above describe, pulled on demand.

How this section works

Tool 1 queries the State of Maryland's SDAT parcel database (via MD iMAP) for two target profiles: motivated homeowners (tired landlords, absentee, long-tenure, trust transfers) and development land (near-vacant lots). Tool 2 queries Montgomery County's live permit system for building activity. Tool 3 pulls housing-code violations. All three are free public records with the owner's mailing address included automatically — a free skip-trace of where to reach them. To resolve phone and email, each result has a Skip Trace button (paid Tracerfy lookup — see the bar below).

Skip Trace settings & credits Not connected

Skip tracing resolves an owner's phone numbers and emails via Tracerfy. This is a paid, per-record service (one lookup can return several people and cost several credits), so it never runs automatically or in bulk — only when you click Skip Trace on a specific lead, with a confirmation.

Security: your token lives only in this browser's localStorage — it is never written into the site's public code or committed to GitHub. Anyone using this tool must paste their own token. Because you shared this token in chat, consider rotating it in your Tracerfy dashboard once set up here.

Credits: lookups are billed per person/contact found, not per address — the counter above tracks spend this browser. Compliance: Tracerfy returns DNC and litigator flags — never call a DNC or litigator number; those are surfaced on every result.

Marketing Sequences & Templates

Luxury sellers convert on touch four, not touch one. Every lead gets a sequence matched to its source — and nothing EEN sends looks like investor mail.

How this system works

The brand rule: discretion, certainty, speed, as-is — never "we buy houses." Physical mail only on heavy stock with a real signature and a direct founder phone line; no postcards, no SMS blasts (TCPA risk), cold calls only on DNC-scrubbed Hot-tier leads. The 6-touch cadence below runs over ~6 months; the three templates cover the highest-value situations. Personalize every bracket before sending, then log the touch date in the Pipeline Tracker.

Guardrails

Maryland Compliance — § 10-715

Maryland's wholesale disclosure law (effective October 1, 2025) gives the other party rescission rights if you skip a disclosure. On a $1M contract, that is an existential risk. Run this checklist on every deal.

How this checklist works

Md. Real Prop. § 10-715 applies to wholesale assignments of owner-occupied residential property of four or fewer units. It requires written disclosure to the seller before the purchase contract is signed, and written disclosure to your end buyer before the assignment is completed. Work through every item before contract and again before assignment; progress saves in this browser. Reset it for each new deal.

Not legal advice. This checklist is an internal operating aid. Have a Maryland real estate attorney draft and review the purchase contract, assignment agreement, and both § 10-715 disclosure forms before first use — and confirm whether novation or double closing changes the disclosure posture on a given deal.
